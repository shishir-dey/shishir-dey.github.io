Economic outcomes are dependent on the frame of reference from which they are evaluated. Winning, losing, zero-sum, and positive-sum are not necessarily intrinsic properties of an economic interaction; they depend on the information, expectations, objectives, time horizon, and variables included in the frame. Information asymmetry creates disagreement because different parties operate with different models of reality, and new information can fundamentally change the perceived outcome without changing the underlying event. Therefore, economic reality and perceived economic reality must be distinguished: the same interaction can appear beneficial, neutral, or harmful under different frames. Zero-sum and positive-sum classifications are consequently incomplete unless the frame of reference and payoff function are explicitly defined.
